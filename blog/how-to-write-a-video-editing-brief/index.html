How to write a video editing brief your editor will love

A clear brief is the fastest way to get the edit you imagined on the first pass. Here is what to include, and what to leave out.

CMBy the CrawlMedia team

Video editing

Most revision rounds don’t happen because an editor got something wrong. They happen because the editor had to guess. A good brief removes the guessing, so the first cut lands closer to what you had in mind and your revision rounds go into polish instead of direction.

Start with the goal, not the footage

Before you describe cuts or transitions, say what the video needs to do. Is it meant to drive sign-ups, explain a product, or keep an existing audience engaged? One sentence on purpose shapes every decision an editor makes, from pacing to music.

What every brief should include

  • The platform and format: for example, a 9:16 reel for Instagram or a 16:9 YouTube upload.
  • Target length, and whether that is a hard limit or a guide.
  • Your audience, in a sentence or two.
  • Two or three reference videos you like, with a note on what you like about each.
  • Brand assets: logo files, fonts, colours, and any intro or outro you always use.
  • Must-have moments, such as a key quote, a product shot, or a call to action.

Organise your footage before you send it

Name files clearly and group them into folders by scene or topic. If you recorded several takes, mark the ones you prefer. An editor working through organised footage spends their time on storytelling rather than searching.

The best briefs are short. One page that answers the right questions beats five pages of notes.

Give feedback in one consolidated round

When the first cut arrives, gather everyone’s comments into a single list with timestamps. Conflicting notes from different people in separate messages are the most common reason projects slow down.

A simple template to copy

  • Goal: what should viewers do or feel after watching?
  • Format and length: platform, aspect ratio, duration.
  • Audience: who is this for?
  • References: links and what to borrow from each.
  • Assets: where to find footage, logos, fonts and music.
  • Deadline: when you need the first cut and the final file.

Fill that in before every project and you will see fewer surprises, faster turnarounds, and edits that feel like your brand from the first draft.
