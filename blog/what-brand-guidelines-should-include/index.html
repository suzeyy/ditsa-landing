What your brand guidelines should include (and what they shouldn’t)

Brand guidelines only work if people actually use them. Keep yours focused on the decisions your team makes every day.

CMBy the CrawlMedia team

Branding

A brand guideline document exists for one reason: to help other people make decisions that look and sound like you, without asking you every time. The most useful guidelines are the ones a busy team member can open, scan, and act on in a couple of minutes.

The essentials

  • Logo usage: primary and alternate versions, minimum sizes, clear space, and a few examples of what not to do.
  • Colour palette: exact values for screen and print, with guidance on which colours lead and which support.
  • Typography: the typefaces, the weights you actually use, and a simple hierarchy for headings and body text.
  • Imagery: the style of photography or illustration that fits, with examples.
  • Tone of voice: a few words that describe how you sound, plus before and after examples.

Show, don’t just tell

Rules are easier to follow when people can see them applied. Include real examples such as a social post, a slide, and an email header, so your team has a pattern to copy rather than a set of abstract instructions.

If a rule needs a paragraph to explain, it probably needs an example instead.

What to leave out

Long brand origin stories, every possible edge case, and rules nobody will enforce all make a guideline harder to use. Keep strategy documents separate and let the guidelines focus on practical, everyday decisions.

Make it easy to find

Store the guidelines alongside the files people need: logo packs, templates, and fonts, in one shared folder. A guideline that lives in someone’s downloads folder will not get used.

Review it once or twice a year. As your brand grows, add examples from real work so the document keeps reflecting how you actually show up.
